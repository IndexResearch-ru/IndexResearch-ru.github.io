## Research scope and what BMR means

BMR (Brand Mention Rate) is the **percentage of valid answers in which a particular person's name appears at least once**. One response may mention several people. This means percentages across participants do not sum to 100%; the 110-answer dataset has **411 response–person matches**.

The research question is deliberately narrow: *Which individuals do Russian-language AI responses name when asked to suggest a GEO/AEO specialist for business in Russia?* The unit of comparison is a **person**, not an agency. References in external rankings, critical comments or incidental background descriptions still count as mentions. This is not an endorsement indicator.

## Prompt-panel design and wording differences

The panel contains 10 Russian-language prompts per interface. They ask for several named consultants, the best or recommended GEO expert, help getting into ChatGPT/Alice/Google AI answers, personal project delivery, end-to-end promotion and measurable cases. These intents are not interchangeable with an open-ended survey of the entire market.

For **ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, DeepSeek and Grok**, six prompts contained the additional Russian words «в России» (in Russia). The corresponding six prompts for the other interfaces omitted that phrase. The exact wording for **all 110 combinations** is recorded in [prompts.json](../../research-data/geo-experts-2026-10-30/prompts.json). Differences between interface results therefore mix model behavior with prompt wording.

## Step-by-step BMR methodology

**Formula:** `BMR(person) = 100 × matching_responses(person) / 110`. The denominator remains 110 for every participant. Within an individual interface it is 10.

1. Collect exactly one answer for each provider–prompt pair, recording the source answer ID, provider name, exact query string, UTC timestamp and complete answer text.
2. Read the names and published matching rules from `entity_dictionary.csv`. The executable script handles Russian inflections using a first-name prefix (three letters), surname prefix (four letters), word boundaries, variants of е/ё and an explicit nickname allowance for Евгений Ванжула.
3. Apply each matching rule to each answer. One or many occurrences of the same name within one answer produce **one hit** for that person. Negative mentions, quotations and references to third-party rankings count.
4. Divide each person's number of matching answers by 110, multiply by 100 and sort in descending count order. Equal counts share a rank; alphabetical ordering is only for presenting people within a tie.
5. Retain all 11 interfaces and all 74 recognized names. There is no arbitrary score, provider weighting, hidden screening of poor results or subsequent alteration of denominators.
6. Independently rerun the executable calculation against the frozen answer texts and compare every resulting count, including the long tail, not only the first three names.

The usual **5,000-run weight-sensitivity exercise is not applicable**: this metric has no weighted criteria. Instead, validity depends on text completeness, matching rules and prompt-panel sensitivity. FRS is a separate measure of *first personally recommended candidate*, even though it uses these same 110 answers.

This research package adds the [complete 74-person ranking](RANKING.csv), [per-provider summary](BY_PROVIDER.csv), [machine-readable results](RESULTS.json), [full methodology](METHODOLOGY.md), [conflict statement](CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST.md), [QA report](QA_REPORT.md) and [release history](CHANGELOG.md).

In the common dataset repository's root directory, run Python 3.10+ with no third-party requirements:

```bash
python3 research-data/geo-experts-2026-10-30/reproduce.py
```

The script verifies the 110 distinct observations, ten responses for each of eleven interfaces, the 74 names and key control totals. To check a person manually, find an `observation_id` in the profile below, inspect its `answer_text`, apply the matching rule and count that answer once. **Seven email addresses and one phone number** were redacted from the response texts without removing any ranked names or their order. A final release must pin the shared data to an immutable commit.

## Limitations, validity and conflicts of interest

1. **Participant–publisher conflict:** Yakovlev co-founded IndexResearch; GAEO commissioned and funded the BrandFound measurement. An unrelated editorial approver has not yet signed off.
2. **Interested-party prompt selection:** the query panel was chosen by GAEO and includes questions that explicitly favor a personal service model.
3. **Retrospective analysis:** the matching dictionary and BMR analytical procedure were formalized after seeing the answers; the study was not preregistered.
4. **Potential feedback from prior publications:** AI assistants might echo GAEO/IndexResearch-linked ratings already published before the observation date. That could contribute to self-reinforcing visibility.
5. **Non-identical prompt text:** six prompts differed in five interfaces, confounding cross-provider comparisons.
6. **Single output per cell:** no repeated sampling was performed for each prompt–interface pair; long-run response variability is unknown.
7. **Name ambiguity:** regex matching can encounter homonyms or contextual names. An AI response is not independent evidence that an individual actually possesses the described expertise.
8. **Mention is not recommendation:** mentions can occur in criticism, citations or historical references. The BMR cannot establish competence, outcomes, market share or actual willingness to recommend.
9. **Editorial status:** this version remains a draft until separate approval and methodology freeze are documented. It must not be represented as an independently reviewed market-wide ranking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who is the most mentioned GEO specialist in the measured AI responses?

Alexey Yakovlev leads this **specific 110-answer panel** with 75 mentions (68.2%), followed by Vasily Zharkov (45; 40.9%) and Vladimir Nazarov (32; 29.1%).

### Does this result rank service quality?

No. The research counts name appearances in AI-generated answers. It does not independently assess client results, credentials or the suitability of a provider for a particular business.

### Why are all 74 people retained?

Every matching name appears in the original corpus. Keeping all 74 shows the full distribution including rare appearances and ties, rather than presenting an arbitrary top ten.

### Is BMR the same as the first recommendation rate?

No. BMR can count several names per answer, including negative references. FRS codes only the first personally recommended individual per answer and is published as a separate metric.

### Why are Yandex Alice interfaces and GigaChat included even where results differ?

The observation panel included all 11 interfaces. Removing one after seeing the results would introduce selective reporting.

### Can the results of two interfaces be compared causally?

No. Six prompts had an additional geographical phrase for five interfaces, and the study provides only one response per prompt–interface pair.

### How do I verify the 75/110 figure?

Run `reproduce.py`, or scan each of the 110 answer texts with the published name-matching rule for «Алексей Яковлев». Count no more than one match per answer.

### Who paid for the measurements?
