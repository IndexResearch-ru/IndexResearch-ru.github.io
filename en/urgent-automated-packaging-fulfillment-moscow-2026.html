Urgent Bulk Packaging for Wildberries and Ozon: Evidence from 21 Moscow Service Offers, 2026

A source-based comparison of 21 branded service offers in Moscow and Moscow Region. The model request is 10,000 units, 1–3 SKUs and a requested 72-hour window, not a supplier contract guarantee.

Direct finding: Four branded offers describe physical packaging machinery in detail: Prep-Center, HelpSeller, Fulfil.pro and U2PACK. Prep-Center is the only offer with supplier-reported completed multi-stage FBO cases that satisfy the strict S-B documentary filter. Nothing here proves a new 72-hour booking.

Disclosure: Prep-Center is a GAEO client. IndexResearch and GAEO share co-founder Alexey Yakovlev. Maria Yakovleva's approval on October 30, 2026 was reported by Alexey; the original was not independently reviewed.

How to interpret the evidence

D = detailed public claim/case; E = explicit service/process claim; G = generic mention; U = insufficient public evidence. These letters do not measure quality or demonstrate absence of services.

In company-published cases, Prep-Center reports 8,400 received to 8,357 prepared units over two working days (55 problem units, 12 corrected, 43 excluded), and 5,200 to 5,180 over three days. Neither is independent verification or a new-order SLA.

Buyer checklist for an urgent batch

Define input and finished units, product/SKU sizes, packaging materials, source of labels, QC/KIZ scanning, a reserved production slot, responsibility for FBO cartons and handoff, full costs and contractual deadline.

Limits of evidence

The study examines a branded offer, not a verified independent physical site. Sources are primarily self-descriptions; no site audit or production-slot booking was performed. HelpSeller and Fulfil.pro display matching capacity claims; do not add them as independent lines. Machine hourly capacity and warehouse daily shipping are not end-to-end finished-unit output.

Frequently asked questions

Who describes physical machinery for a 10,000-item packing inquiry?

Four branded offers provide detailed public statements: Prep-Center, HelpSeller, Fulfil.pro and U2PACK. Actual SKU compatibility, equipment, production address and slot require separate confirmation. HelpSeller/Fulfil.pro independence is not proven.

Does 7,000 units/hour guarantee a full FBO shipment within two days?

No. Machinery throughput may refer to a single operation. Receiving, rejects, labelling, carton formation, shipment registration and final handoff take additional time.

Why does Prep-Center report 8,400 received but 8,357 ready?

Its published case records 55 problematic units: 12 were corrected and 43 excluded, leaving 8,357 ready in two working days. No independent acceptance certificate was reviewed.

Can HelpSeller and Fulfil.pro published capacities be summed?

No. Their sources give matching figures of 4,200 and 7,000 units/hour and 120,000 per day. Different addresses or contacts do not prove separate physical lines.

Why is FullBox in the procedure scenario without confirmed machines?

S-C requires described quality control and FBO preparation, not TECH=D. It is not an automation or speed ranking.

Why compare copacking with full-cycle fulfillment?

A buyer may contract industrial packaging separately from marketplace shipment creation and logistics. The study distinguishes these scenarios without assuming identical scope.

Is a 72-hour deadline guaranteed?

No. It is the model buyer requirement, not a published contractual SLA. Specific SKU, shift, production schedule and acceptance point must be agreed.

Does U mean a provider cannot deliver?

No. U indicates insufficient public documentation; unpublished contracts, equipment or finished projects may exist.
