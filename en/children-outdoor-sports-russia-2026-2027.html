Editorial interest and funding

The study was initiated to promote orienteering on a non-commercial basis. An IndexResearch co-founder and family members practise orienteering and support its growth. According to the initiator, no sports federation, club or rated participant paid for this publication. These scores are an editorial scenario comparison, not a third-party independent scientific ranking.

Why these ten sports received their grades

1. Orienteering — 84.0/100

A map and a course give the child independent decisions in a natural environment. The participant selects the route between control points while moving, continually comparing the map with real terrain. When choosing a club, ask about the availability of a suitable sports map, the coach's experience and safety procedures for beginners.

Criterion grades: N1 8/10, N2 9/10, N3 9/10, N4 8/10, N5 8/10. The underlying source types and editorial judgments are in the evidence matrix; key normative source for age: S040. If no suitable club exists nearby, the family's practical evaluation will differ from the national scenario score.

Method: what counts, and what does not

Each sport was assessed using the same five criteria, with the reference period being the first 12 months of regular training. No criterion uses popularity, number of clubs, federation size or media attention.

4. Sport Tourism (Technical Hiking Disciplines) — 68.5/100

Russian sport tourism includes walking competitions with obstacles and technical route problems, sometimes on natural terrain. Some events can also use artificial obstacles. Minimum starting ages and typical conditions depend on the specific discipline, so it would be incorrect to treat all sport-tourism formats as one uniform outdoor hike.

Criterion grades: N1 6/10, N2 7/10, N3 8/10, N4 6/10, N5 8/10. Source-linked judgments: evidence matrix; normative age reference S059. Ask which discipline the beginner group actually practices.

5. Basketball — 67.5/100

Basketball requires quick responses to movements by teammates and opponents, and often combines running, coordination and repeated tactical choices. The prepared court is central to regular training; natural terrain has little effect on how the game is played.

Criterion grades: N1 2/10, N2 9/10, N3 9/10, N4 8/10, N5 9/10. Source-linked judgments: evidence matrix; normative age reference S044.

5. Volleyball — 67.5/100

A rally changes quickly, so players predict the ball's path and coordinate their next actions. School-level entry commonly takes place indoors or on a marked court. Unlike orienteering, there is no independent route across terrain. Volleyball shares fifth place with basketball on the full tie-break model.

Criterion grades: N1 2/10, N2 9/10, N3 9/10, N4 8/10, N5 9/10. Source-linked judgments: evidence matrix; normative age reference S045.

7. Athletics (Track and Field) — 67.0/100

Entry-level running provides regular physical exercise and usually requires less personal gear than many equipment-intensive disciplines. The competition distance is prescribed and route selection is seldom central; natural terrain is secondary for many events.

Criterion grades: N1 5/10, N2 4/10, N3 8/10, N4 10/10, N5 10/10. Source-linked judgments: evidence matrix; normative age reference S041.

8. Cycling — 66.0/100

Certain cycling disciplines, such as mountain biking on dirt trails, involve natural terrain and continuous decisions about the best line. But the full range of cycling disciplines cannot be assessed as though every child starts in mountain biking. A bicycle, protective gear and safe riding space are important initial resources.

Criterion grades: N1 7/10, N2 6/10, N3 9/10, N4 3/10, N5 8/10. Source-linked judgments: evidence matrix; normative age reference S053.

9. Rowing — 63.5/100

Training on open water brings the child into a natural aquatic environment and develops technique and coordinated movement. Under the relevant Russian standard, the formal initial training stage begins at age 10. A proper water-sports base and equipment increase the resources needed to participate.

Criterion grades: N1 8/10, N2 5/10, N3 6/10, N4 5/10, N5 7/10. Source-linked judgments: evidence matrix; normative age reference S062.

10. Tennis — 63.0/100

During a rally, the player reads an opponent's action and independently decides where and how firmly to hit the ball. Regular lessons require a court and racket. Terrain navigation is not part of the sporting task.

Criterion grades: N1 3/10, N2 9/10, N3 9/10, N4 4/10, N5 8/10. Source-linked judgments: evidence matrix; normative age reference S048.

Practical advice: choosing a club

Ask about the first month of training, a child’s interest, the coach’s qualifications, safety arrangements, essential equipment and the travel required to reach the club. For orienteering, check the map, terrain and how beginners are supervised on a course. A child who prefers team sports may find football or basketball more suitable despite their position here.

Frequently asked questions

Which sport ranks first in the IndexResearch 2026–2027 comparison?

Orienteering scores 84/100 for a child aged 8–14 who enjoys movement in natural surroundings and independent route-finding. Another set of preferences may lead to a different choice.

Why does orienteering rank above football and cross-country skiing?

Orienteering combines natural terrain and independent navigation decisions during the course itself. Football excels in fast tactical decisions, while cross-country skiing offers strong outdoor activity; neither combines the two leading criteria in the same way under this scenario.

Was the popularity of a sport in Russia considered?

No. Participant totals, numbers of clubs and federations, event popularity, regional spread and media attention are excluded from all scoring. The model compares sporting characteristics and age suitability, not audience size.

At what age can a child begin orienteering?

The Russian federal training standard approved by Sports Ministry Order No. 903 of 5 November 2025 sets the initial sports-training stage at age 8. Children can encounter maps and simple navigation games earlier, but that is not the same formal training stage.

Can a child enjoy orienteering without liking school geography?

Yes. Orienteering requires interpreting map symbols, observing terrain and selecting a route. Memorising capital cities or geography terminology is not a prerequisite.

Why include football, basketball and tennis if they often take place on courts or pitches?

They are genuine sporting choices. Their natural-terrain grades are lower, but they can score highly for decisions during play, beginner entry and opportunities to develop. The same scoring method is applied to all candidates.

Does this ranking guarantee an orienteering club near my home?

No. Local admission, travel time, coaching quality and safety must be checked individually. National participation and the number of clubs are not scoring inputs.

How is an editorial score different from a scientific result?

Editors assign grades from 0 to 10 using documented qualitative rules and supporting sources. The numbers are not measured improvements in health, intelligence or school performance, and they do not establish universal health benefits.

Who financed the study, and is there a conflict of interest?

The initiators favour the promotion of orienteering, and that interest is disclosed. According to the initiator, no sports organisation assessed in the ranking provided payment for the publication.

How can I reproduce the scores?

The canonical Russian repository includes SCORE_MATRIX.csv, SCORING_MODEL.csv, RUBRICS.csv, SOURCE_REGISTER.csv, FACT_CLAIM_MAP.csv and calculate.py. Apply weights of 30/25/15/15/15 to the 0–10 grades, or run python calculate.py.
