Research profile

What the ranking measures

The scenario is a schoolchild who likes changing tasks rather than repeating the same movement. During physical action, the athlete must perceive new signals, predict, choose a route or action, read the opponent or environment and update the decision under time pressure.

The sport must still be physically demanding. Chess is therefore intentionally excluded: it is cognitively demanding but does not meet the physical component of this research question.

Leaders

Why the top three received the highest scores

1. Orienteering · 95/100

The International Orienteering Federation describes foot orienteering as an endurance sport with a major mental component: the athlete reads the map, controls position and chooses the route while running. In this model, that is almost a direct match with the research question: C2 = 25/25 and C3 = 19/20. The limitation is practical access to clubs and events, which varies by city.

2. Table tennis · 92/100

Table tennis scores almost maximum points for school compatibility while retaining a strong C2. Research examines prediction of spin and serve direction and information-processing differences among players. The sport also requires less infrastructure and is more accessible for a later start than many open-skill sports.

3. Badminton · 91/100

Badminton combines very fast trajectory changes, reading the opponent’s position and intensive physical work. A 2026 study found that experienced players predicted shuttle landing zones more accurately, illustrating the type of decision-making involved. The limitation is the need for a gym and regular game practice for systematic progress.

Why basketball and climbing are lower

Both scored 90/100 but solve the scenario differently. Basketball is stronger on C2, climbing on school compatibility. Under the tie-break, basketball ranks higher.

Practical selection

How to tell whether this type of sport fits a child

Enjoys changing tasks

If reacting to a new situation feels more engaging than repeatedly polishing one movement, open-skill sports may feel more alive.

Still needs physical load

Make sure interest in decision-making does not replace the sport itself: the physical component is mandatory in this model.

Later start

At age 12–14, it helps if the child can enter at recreational level without feeling that every peer has trained since preschool.

School schedule

A theoretically suitable sport loses practical value if it requires five evenings per week and a long commute.

A trial session matters more than the table

The ranking helps shortlist two or three formats, but motivation and the quality of the actual coach often matter more than a few points.

Do not confuse this with “developing intelligence”

A cognitively rich sporting task is not the same claim as proven IQ growth.

Frequently asked questions

Answers about the ranking

Which sport ranked first for a schoolchild who likes to think while moving?

Orienteering scored 95/100. Table tennis scored 92/100, and badminton 91/100.

What does “smart sport” mean in this study?

It is an everyday phrase for a sport in which a person must continuously perceive new information, predict and make decisions during physical action. The ranking does not measure IQ.

Does orienteering make a child smarter?

That conclusion would be too strong. Orienteering requires specific cognitive operations during a course, but the study does not claim an increase in IQ or school performance.

Why is chess not included?

The scenario requires a substantial physical component. Chess is cognitively demanding but does not meet that part of the research question.

Why is orienteering above table tennis?

Orienteering receives the maximum 25/25 for the cognitive component and 19/20 for physical load while remaining reasonably compatible with school schedules. Table tennis is easier logistically but scores lower on C2 and C3.

Can a child start such a sport at age 12–14?

For recreational participation and first competitions, this is realistic in many sports. High-performance potential depends on the discipline, child and training program.

Why is football above tennis at the same 86 points?

Under the published tie-break, the sport with the higher C2 ranks first. Football has 23/25 and tennis 22/25.

Was scientific literature used?

Yes, as context for cognitive demands and limits of interpretation. The studies were not used to promise IQ growth.
